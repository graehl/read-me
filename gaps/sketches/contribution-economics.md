# Contribution economics: value, rewards and a public service

Sketched 2026-11-18. Later direction; read-me may grow from a reading
recorder into a public contribution service.

## Premise

Useful speech with reliable transcripts has value to whoever trains
recognizers, and some people will contribute for free (the impulse behind
volunteer computing projects), others for payment, credit or access to
better models. The aim is more and better speech data with informed consent
and a fair exchange, without complaints a contributor could legitimately
make.

## Account for usable data, not minutes

Value is per accepted, permitted, usable contribution after review,
deduplication and fraud loss. Costs: inference (recognizers, any AI partner),
storage, review, support and payouts. A reward budget is set from that
accounting, never promised in advance as unlimited service.

## What makes speech valuable

For speech recognition the gate is a sincere attempt to communicate in a
wanted language, plus identifiable review of the transcript. Expertise in
the topic does not matter; accents, code-switching, disfluency and unusual
voices add coverage. Keep separate: acoustic diversity, label reliability,
permission compatibility and fraud risk. (Problem-solving text or tool
traces would add task competence as a further gate; not this project's
data.)

## Rewards and feedback

- Lead with an activity that is useful or enjoyable by itself (reading for a
  language you care about, reviewing, dictation you would do anyway), then
  the contribution ask. Avoid market-research framing.
- Publish an understandable deal when one exists. No pressure to disclose,
  no penalties for deleting or withdrawing before release, and never reward
  embarrassing or sensitive content.
- Feedback can tell a contributor their work matches a requested kind of
  data; claim it improved a model only after an actual accepted use.
- Visible ratings are open: they invite gaming and complaints but also pride
  and sharing. A person is not globally good or bad; quality is per task and
  language. Never expose fraud thresholds.

## Demand

Consumers of the data can request a language, register, quality tier or
task kind. Route requests to contributors whose permissions and choices
match; requests never grant identity or raw-data access, and requests aimed
at a specific contributor need bounds so they do not become targeting. Turn
demand into a better offer (a funded task, a better activity), not a
fabricated progress meter.

## Service shape

- Open, copyable contribution terms and record formats
  ([consent](contribution-consent.md)) so other tools and harnesses can feed
  the same pool, and contributors can carry their preferences between them.
- Free or subsidized access to models (for dictation, tutoring or play) in
  exchange for contributions, possibly through data-sharing arrangements
  with inference providers who want better speech models.
- Trust and low signup friction matter more than technical moat.

## Open

- Legal entity, terms, payout mechanics, tax.
- Pricing per language and task kind; how to fund reviewers.
- Abuse at public scale: [contribution abuse](contribution-abuse.md).
